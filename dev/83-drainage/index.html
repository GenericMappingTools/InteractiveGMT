Drainage analysis

Grid Tools → Drainage analysis… — the green ? at the bottom of the dialog opens this page. For a worked example, step by step, see the tutorial.

From a digital elevation model alone, this tool works out where the water goes: the drainage basins (catchments), the river network with its Strahler order, the flow accumulation, the topographic wetness index, the distance to the outlet along the flow, and the drain directions in the form hydrological models such as LISFLOOD read. The computation is done by GMT.jl's hydrology functions (flowdir, flowacc, drainagebasins, streamnet, streamorder, twi, flowdistance, ldd).

It works on the grid on display in the window. In a window with no grid the dialog still opens, with every control greyed out until a grid is loaded.

Depressions

Real DEMs are full of small pits — cells lower than all their neighbours, mostly noise — where water would stop and the basins would break into thousands of fragments. They are removed first:

  • Fill raises each pit to the level of its lowest rim. Robust; the filled hollows become flat areas, and the flow on them is routed toward their spill point.
  • Carve keeps each pit at its own elevation and lowers a narrow channel from it through its rim. It changes fewer cells and suits DEMs where a road or a dam blocks a valley.

z ≤ 0 is sea (no data)

Cells at or below zero are treated as sea, so rivers end at the coast. Keep it ticked for DEMs that include bathymetry, like @earth_relief; untick it for land below sea level (the Dead Sea, polders) or a DEM on a different datum.

Results

Tick what you want. Each one is added to the window as a new layer, named after the source grid ("… drainage basins", "… wetness index (TWI, MFD)", …):

ResultWhat it is
Drainage basinsevery cell labelled with the basin it drains to; 1 = the largest. Basins smaller than the min. area are lumped into one class, 0 = coastal / residual drainage (light grey): along a coast, the many small catchments that run straight into the sea instead of joining a river. The basin limits also come as lines on top of the display ("… basin outlines"), like the rivers
Stream network (lines)the rivers as lines on top of the display, one line per reach
Strahler ordera grid with the order of each river cell
Flow accumulationlog10 of the upstream area draining through each cell (km²)
Wetness index (TWI)ln(a / tan β): where water collects and stays
Flow distance to outletdistance along the flow path to the basin outlet (km)
Drain directions (LDD)flow direction of each cell, PCRaster keypad code (8 = north, 5 = outlet)

min. area — the smallest basin kept, and the upstream area at which a river starts (the stream network and the Strahler order use the second one).

areas in — the unit of the two min. areas: cells, or km² for a georeferenced DEM (geographic, or projected in metres).

Flow method (two boxes) — how water is passed downhill for the flow accumulation and for the wetness index:

  • D8 sends all the water of a cell to its steepest neighbour. Every cell belongs to exactly one flow path, which is what basins, rivers and LDD need — those results always use D8. On smooth hillslopes it draws artificial parallel lines.
  • MFD (multiple flow direction) shares the water among all lower neighbours, in proportion to their slope. Smooth and realistic on hillslopes, but it makes rivers several cells wide.
  • D∞ splits the water between the two neighbours that bracket the steepest direction. Exact on planar slopes, less spreading than MFD.

For the accumulation map as a picture of the rivers, use D8. For the wetness index and anything about hillslopes (soil moisture, erosion, landslide susceptibility), use MFD — the usual choice — or D∞.

Saving a river with its upstream network

Right-click a river in the view and choose Save this river and its upstream network…. What is saved is that river — from the point you clicked down to its next junction — plus every river that flows into it, and every river that flows into those, up to the sources: the whole drainage network above that point. One segment per reach, each with its Strahler order.

The file type follows the extension: a GMT multisegment table (.txt, .dat, .gmt: x, y, z and order, one > segment per reach) or a GeoPackage, Shapefile, KML or GeoJSON (the order is a field of each line).

Painting each river system in its own colour

Right-click the rivers (in the view or on their Scene Objects row) and tick Paint each river system in its own colour. A river system is a river that reaches the sea (or the edge of the grid) together with every tributary that drains into it; each one gets a colour of its own. Untick it to put the whole network back in one colour. Picking a colour in Line properties does the same: the network takes the picked colour as a whole.

Run and Stop

Run computes the ticked results. The bar and the line under it follow the computation (Filling depressions, Flow directions, Flow accumulation…); the window stays usable meanwhile. Stop ends the run, and nothing is added.

Closing the dialog (the X, minimise or Esc) parks it in the window's Scene Objects, settings intact: double-click its row to bring it back, or use the row's Delete to end it. A run going on when it is parked carries on, and its results are added as usual.

Almost all the time goes into removing the depressions. Measured times:

DEM sizeDepressions + directionsBasinsMFD accumulation
0.75 M cells0.3 s< 0.1 s0.2 s
25 M cells (5000 × 5000)3 s0.4 s5 s
100 M cells (10000 × 10000)16 s2 s24 s

Memory: about 40 bytes per cell on top of the DEM (4 GB for 100 M cells).

In the console

The same functions are at hand in the window's Julia Console, with options the dialog does not show:

  • drainagebasins(F, outlets=[lon lat], snap=n) — the basin above a given point;
  • flowdir(G, sinks=M) — keep the cells of mask M as real sinks (endorheic lakes, karst);
  • flowacc(F, weights=P) — accumulate a quantity per cell (rainfall, runoff) instead of area;
  • flowacc(F, method=:mfd, p=…) — the MFD exponent: ≈1 spreads widely, large values approach D8;
  • twi(F, minslope=…) — the slope floor for flat cells (default 0.001).

Each function's help (?flowdir, ?flowacc, …) lists all its options.
