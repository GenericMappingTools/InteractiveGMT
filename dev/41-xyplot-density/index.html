density — Kernel Density Estimate

The density icon on the X,Y Plot tool's gallery row estimates the probability density of the Y values of every series of the current page, with a normal kernel, and draws one density curve per series. The curves go on a new page; the series they were made from stay as they were. The numbers come from GMT.jl's own kernelDensity, the helper behind GMT.jl's density.

Syntax

Draw the samples as curves, then click density:

p = plot(y)
plot!(p, y2)          # more samples: one density curve each

Description

  • Each series of the page is one sample: its Y values. Its X values are not used.
  • The estimate uses a normal (Gaussian) kernel. The bandwidth h is Silverman's rule of thumb, h = 0.9 · min(σ, IQR/1.34) · n^(-1/5), with σ the sample's standard deviation, IQR its interquartile range and n its number of values.
  • The density is evaluated at 200 points, evenly spaced from the sample's minimum to its maximum. It is not extended past them, so a curve can end above zero at either side.
  • Each curve is named "<series> density", in the next colour of the palette. The new page is called density, its axes value and density.
  • Values that are not finite (NaN, Inf) are left out. A series with fewer than two values is skipped; if none has two, the icon says so and draws nothing.

Running the Examples

Paste each example into the iGMT window's Julia console, or into the Julia REPL after using InteractiveGMT, then click density on the gallery row. No series needs to be selected: the icon takes every series of the page.

The examples build their samples without random numbers: mod.((1:n) .* 0.6180339887, 1) gives n values spread evenly over (0, 1) in no particular order, and a distribution's inverse CDF turns them into a sample of that distribution.

Examples

Estimate the Density of a Sample

500 values of a logistic distribution.

u = mod.((1:500) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
y = log.(u ./ (1 .- u))
p = plot(y; legend="sample")

Density of Bimodal Data

Two clusters, around -2 and 3: the density shows both modes.

u = mod.((1:400) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
z = log.(u ./ (1 .- u))
y = [-2 .+ 0.5 .* z[1:200]; 3 .+ z[201:400]]
p = plot(y; legend="two modes")

Compare the Densities of Two Samples

Each series of the page gets its own curve, on the same axes.

u = mod.((1:300) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
p = plot(-log.(1 .- u); legend="exponential")
plot!(p, 4 .* u; legend="uniform")

Input Arguments

y — sample, the Y values of a series: at least two finite numbers.
