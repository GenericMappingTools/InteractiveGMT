marginalhist — Scatter Plot with Marginal Histograms

The marginalhist icon on the X,Y Plot tool's gallery row draws the selected series as a scatter plot of its X against its Y, with the histogram of X above it and the histogram of Y on its right. The plot goes on a new page; the series it was made from stays as it was. It follows GMT.jl's marginalhist (MATLAB calls this a scatter histogram chart).

Syntax

Draw the points as a series, select it in the Object Manager, then click marginalhist:

p = plot(x, y)

Description

  • The points (x, y) of the selected series are drawn as dots in the main, square panel. A point with a coordinate that is not finite is left out. The series needs two or more points.
  • Above the main panel, the histogram of the X values, with its bars rising upwards; on its right, the histogram of the Y values, with its bars pointing to the right. The tallest bar of each fills 95 % of its margin panel. The number of bins follows the Freedman–Diaconis rule (bin width 2·IQR·n^(-1/3)), as the histogram icon does.
  • X and Y each span their range, from the minimum to the maximum rounded out to round numbers; a histogram shares the range of the scatter it stands beside. The two ranges are written along the bottom and the left side of the main panel, whose axes are titled x and y.
  • The page is square and has no X,Y axes of its own. In the Object Manager the panel outlines are one row (panels), the dots another (named after the series), and x histogram and y histogram the margins.

GMT.jl's marginalhist bins the points into hexagons above 2000 points; here they are always drawn as dots.

Running the Examples

Paste each example into the iGMT window's Julia console, or into the Julia REPL after using InteractiveGMT. The series a plot call draws last is the selected one, so click marginalhist on the gallery row right after it; to use another series, click its row in the Object Manager first.

Examples

Create Scatter Plot with Marginal Histograms

Five hundred points of two related quantities.

k = 1:500
x = 3 .* sin.(k)
y = x .+ 2 .* cos.(2.3 .* k)
p = plot(x, y; ls=:none, marker=:circle, legend="samples")

Choose the Series

Two series on the page: the last one drawn, B, is the selected one. Click A in the Object Manager before the icon to draw its histograms instead.

k = 1:300
p = plot(sin.(1.1 .* k), cos.(0.7 .* k); ls=:none, marker=:circle, legend="A")
plot!(p, 2 .+ sin.(k) .^ 3, 0.5 .* k ./ 300 .+ 0.3 .* sin.(2.9 .* k); ls=:none, marker=:circle, legend="B")

Marginal Histograms of a Table

A table drawn with plot is its second column against its first: those are the scatter's Y and X.

depth = range(0, 1000; length=200)
temp  = 4 .+ 16 .* exp.(-depth ./ 200) .+ 0.5 .* sin.(depth ./ 7)
D = mat2ds([depth temp], colnames=["depth", "temperature"])
p = plot(D)

Input Arguments

x — X values, the X coordinates of the selected series' points.

y — Y values, the Y coordinates of the selected series' points, one per X.
