ecdfplot — Empirical Cumulative Distribution Plot

The ecdfplot icon on the X,Y Plot tool's gallery row draws the empirical cumulative distribution function (ECDF) of the Y values of every series of the current page, as stairs, one per series. The curves go on a new page; the series they were made from stay as they were. The numbers come from GMT.jl's own ecdf, the helper behind GMT.jl's ecdfplot.

Syntax

Draw the samples as curves, then click ecdfplot:

p = plot(y)
plot!(p, y2)          # more samples: one ECDF each

Description

  • Each series of the page is one sample: its Y values. Its X values are not used.
  • The ECDF F(v) is the fraction of the sample's values that are ≤ v. It is drawn as stairs: it starts at 0 just left of the smallest value, rises by 1/n at each value (n the number of values) and reaches 1 at the largest.
  • Each curve is named "<series> ECDF", in the next colour of the palette. The new page is called ECDF, its axes value and cumulative probability.
  • Values that are not finite (NaN, Inf) are left out. A series with fewer than two values is skipped; if none has two, the icon says so and draws nothing.

Running the Examples

Paste each example into the iGMT window's Julia console, or into the Julia REPL after using InteractiveGMT, then click ecdfplot on the gallery row. No series needs to be selected: the icon takes every series of the page.

The examples build their samples without random numbers: mod.((1:n) .* 0.6180339887, 1) gives n values spread evenly over (0, 1) in no particular order, and a distribution's inverse CDF turns them into a sample of that distribution.

Examples

Create ECDF Plot

50 values of a logistic distribution.

u = mod.((1:50) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
p = plot(log.(u ./ (1 .- u)); legend="sample")

Compare Two Samples

Each series of the page gets its own stairs, on the same axes: the uniform sample's ECDF is close to a straight line, the exponential one's rises fast and then flattens.

u = mod.((1:40) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
p = plot(2 .* u; legend="uniform")
plot!(p, -log.(1 .- u); legend="exponential")

ECDF of a Sequence

The sines of the integers 1 to 200 fill [-1, 1], most densely near its ends: the ECDF is steep at both sides and flat in the middle.

y = sin.(1:200)
p = plot(y; legend="sin(k)")

Input Arguments

y — sample, the Y values of a series: at least two finite numbers.
