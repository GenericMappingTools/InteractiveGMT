compassplot — Arrows from the Origin

The compassplot icon on the X,Y Plot tool's gallery row draws each point of each series of the current page as an arrow from the centre of polar axes: its X value is the arrow's angle, in radians, and its Y value its length. The plot goes on a new page; the curves it was made from stay as they were.

Syntax

Draw the angles and lengths as curves, then click compassplot:

p = plot(theta, rho)
plot!(p, theta2, rho2)          # more sets of arrows

Description

  • Each point (θ, ρ) becomes an arrow from the centre to that point, with a head at its end. The arrows of one series share its colour.
  • θ is in radians, counted counter-clockwise from the +X direction (0 points right).
  • ρ is the arrow's length. A negative length is drawn at its absolute value; a zero-length arrow is not drawn.
  • The polar axes: circles at a quarter, a half, three quarters and all of the longest arrow (rounded up to a round number), labelled with their values; spokes and angle labels every 30°.
  • The page is square and has no X,Y axes of its own.

To draw vectors given by their components u and v, give atan.(v, u) as the angles and hypot.(u, v) as the lengths.

Running the Examples

Paste each example into the iGMT window's Julia console, or into the Julia REPL after using InteractiveGMT, then click compassplot on the gallery row.

Examples

Create Compass Plot

Three arrows, at 45°, 90° and 135°.

theta = [π/4, π/2, 3π/4]
rho = [1, 2, 3]
p = plot(theta, rho)

Plot Vectors from Their Components

u = [3, -1, -2, 1, 2.5]
v = [1, 2, -1.5, -2.5, 2]
p = plot(atan.(v, u), hypot.(u, v); legend="vectors")

Compare Two Sets of Arrows

theta = range(0, 2π; length=9)[1:8]
p = plot(theta, fill(2.0, 8); legend="unit")
plot!(p, theta .+ 0.2, [1.3, 1.8, 1.5, 1.9, 1.2, 1.6, 1.4, 1.7]; legend="measured")

Input Arguments

theta — arrow angles, in radians, as the series' X values.

rho — arrow lengths, as the series' Y values, one per angle.
