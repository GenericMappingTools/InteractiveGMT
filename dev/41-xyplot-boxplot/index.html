boxplot — Box Chart

The boxplot icon on the X,Y Plot tool's gallery row draws the Y values of every series of the current page as a box chart: one box per series, with its median, quartiles, whiskers and outliers. The boxes go on a new page; the series they were made from stay as they were. The numbers come from GMT.jl's own helper2_boxplot, the helper behind GMT.jl's boxplot.

Syntax

Draw the samples as curves, then click boxplot:

p = plot(y)
plot!(p, y2)                          # more samples: one box each
p = plot(x, Y; multicol=true)         # or one box per column of a matrix

Description

  • Each series of the page is one sample: its Y values. Its X values are not used.
  • The boxes stand at X = 1, 2, 3, ..., in the order of the series, each in its own colour and named after its series. The new page is called boxplot, its axes series and value.
  • Outliers are the values below Q1 - 1.5·IQR or above Q3 + 1.5·IQR, where Q1 and Q3 are the sample's first and third quartiles and IQR = Q3 - Q1. They are drawn as circles, all in one series named outliers, at the X of their box. With no outlier on the page there is no such series.
  • The box, its median and its whiskers are computed from the values that remain once the outliers are taken out:
    • the box spans the first to the third quartile, 0.5 wide;
    • a line across it marks the median;
    • the whiskers go from the box down to the smallest and up to the largest remaining value, each ending in a cap half as wide as the box.
  • Quartiles and median are Julia's quantile (linear interpolation between the sorted values).
  • Values that are not finite (NaN, Inf) are left out. A series with fewer than two values is skipped; if none has two, the icon says so and draws nothing.

Running the Examples

Paste each example into the iGMT window's Julia console, or into the Julia REPL after using InteractiveGMT, then click boxplot on the gallery row. No series needs to be selected: the icon takes every series of the page.

The examples build their samples without random numbers: mod.((1:n) .* 0.6180339887, 1) gives n values spread evenly over (0, 1) in no particular order, and a distribution's inverse CDF turns them into a sample of that distribution.

Examples

Create Box Chart

100 values of a logistic distribution. Its long tails put a value at each end past the whiskers: those two are the outliers.

u = mod.((1:100) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
p = plot(log.(u ./ (1 .- u)); legend="logistic")

Compare Several Samples

One box per series: a symmetric sample, a flat one and a skewed one.

u = mod.((1:200) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
p = plot(log.(u ./ (1 .- u)); legend="logistic")
plot!(p, 6 .* u .- 3; legend="uniform")
plot!(p, -log.(1 .- u); legend="exponential")

Box Chart of a Matrix's Columns

A year of daily temperatures, cut into four quarters of 90 days, one column each. With multicol=true each column is a series, and so a box.

t = 1:360
T = 15 .+ 8 .* sin.(2π .* (t .- 100) ./ 365) .+ 2 .* sin.(1.7 .* t)
p = plot(1:90, reshape(T, 90, 4); legend="quarter", multicol=true)

Input Arguments

y — sample, the Y values of a series: at least two finite numbers.

Y — samples, a matrix drawn with multicol=true: each column is one series, and one box.
