qqnorm — Quantile-Quantile Plot Against the Normal

The qqnorm icon on the X,Y Plot tool's gallery row plots the quantiles of the Y values of every series of the current page against the quantiles of the standard normal distribution, with an identity line for reference. The plot goes on a new page; the series it was made from stay as they were. It follows GMT.jl's qqnorm (GMT.jl's qqplot(x) with its default qqline=:identity), with the numbers from GMT.jl's own qqbuild and erfinv.

Syntax

Draw the samples as curves, then click qqnorm:

p = plot(y)
plot!(p, y2)          # more samples: one set of points each

Description

  • Each series of the page is one sample: its Y values. Its X values are not used.
  • For a sample of n values, the sorted values are paired with the standard normal quantiles at the probabilities (i - 0.5)/n, i = 1, ..., n. Each pair is one point: X the sample quantile, Y the normal quantile.
  • Each sample's points are drawn as circles, with no line, named after its series, in its own colour. The new page is called qqnorm, its axes sample quantiles and normal quantiles.
  • A dashed black line named identity is the line Y = X, drawn from the smallest to the largest sample quantile on the page. A standard normal sample lies along it. A normal sample with another mean or standard deviation lies along another straight line; a sample that is not normal bends away from a straight line.
  • Values that are not finite (NaN, Inf) are left out. A series with fewer than two values is skipped; if none has two, the icon says so and draws nothing.

Running the Examples

Paste each example into the iGMT window's Julia console, or into the Julia REPL after using InteractiveGMT, then click qqnorm on the gallery row. No series needs to be selected: the icon takes every series of the page.

The examples build their samples without random numbers: mod.((1:n) .* 0.6180339887, 1) gives n values spread evenly over (0, 1) in no particular order, and a distribution's inverse CDF turns them into a sample of that distribution.

Examples

Check a Sample for Normality

A standard normal sample, made with the Box–Muller transform from two such sequences: the points follow the identity line.

k  = 1:300
u1 = mod.(k .* 0.6180339887, 1)
u2 = mod.(k .* 0.4142135624, 1)
z  = sqrt.(-2 .* log.(u1)) .* cos.(2π .* u2)
p  = plot(z; legend="normal")

A Skewed Sample

An exponential sample is not normal: its points bend away from the line.

u = mod.((1:200) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
p = plot(-log.(1 .- u); legend="exponential")

Short and Long Tails

Two samples with mean 0 and variance 1. Against the normal, the uniform one's tails are too short and the logistic one's too long: their points bend away from the line in opposite directions.

u = mod.((1:200) .* 0.6180339887, 1)
p = plot(sqrt(12) .* (u .- 0.5); legend="uniform")
plot!(p, (sqrt(3) / π) .* log.(u ./ (1 .- u)); legend="logistic")

Input Arguments

y — sample, the Y values of a series: at least two finite numbers.
